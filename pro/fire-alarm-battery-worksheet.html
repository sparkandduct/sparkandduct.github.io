Fire alarm battery calculation worksheet

Three US Letter pages: two blank worksheets (use one per panel or power supply) and a filled-in example with a checklist. Print at 100 percent. Fill in device currents from each data sheet, then follow the lettered lines A to J.

The duration used here is the common case: 24 hours of standby followed by 5 minutes of alarm. Voice evacuation, remote station and auxiliary systems, and systems on a generator can need other times. The derating factor is left blank on purpose: write the one your panel's installation manual or the authority having jurisdiction requires.

Next standard size at or above 3.75 Ah: 4.5 Ah, if the panel's charger allows it.

Check before you hand it in

  • □ Strobe current is the figure for the candela setting you will install, not the nominal one.
  • □ Door holders, relays, annunciators and anything else the panel powers are in the table.
  • □ Two 12 V batteries in series make 24 V at the amp-hour rating of one battery.
  • □ Standby and alarm times match the system type and what the authority having jurisdiction requires.
  • □ Charger is listed for a battery this size and the battery fits in the cabinet.

Sources

  • Fire-Lite MS-2 manual, Calculating the Battery Size (ManualsLib): 24 hours standby followed by 5 minutes alarm for local, central and proprietary systems, the sum of standby and alarm amp-hours, and the derating multiplier.
  • Security Sales & Integration: Understanding secondary power supplies: the same 24 hour and 5 minute durations, cited to NFPA 72 (2002 edition in that article).

Last checked: November 2026. The derating factor, voice and mass notification times and remote station times are not stated here because editions of NFPA 72 and manufacturers differ; confirm them against the edition your jurisdiction has adopted.
