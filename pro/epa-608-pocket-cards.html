EPA 608 pocket cards

Four cards on one US Letter page. Print at 100 percent scale, cut on the dashed lines, and laminate or tape each one. Each card is 4 by 5.25 inches. The rules are the federal ones in 40 CFR part 82, subpart F; a state or the equipment maker can require more.

Inches of mercury vacuum, relative to 29.9 in. Hg atmospheric. The date is when the recovery machine was made. Charge size is the full charge of the appliance or isolated part.

Leaks stop you reaching the level: isolate the leaking part, evacuate the rest to the table level, and take the leaking part as low as you can without contaminating the refrigerant. That level may not be above 0 psig.

Not a major repair and no evacuation to the atmosphere afterward: high- and very high-pressure appliances go to 0 psig before opening. Low-pressure appliances are pressurized to 0 psig.

Small appliances

Definition: factory manufactured, charged and hermetically sealed with 5 pounds or less of refrigerant. Examples: household refrigerators and freezers, window and portable air conditioners, dehumidifiers, under-the-counter ice makers, vending machines, drinking water coolers.

Recover, before opening or disposing, with a certified machine, one of:

Machine madeCompressorRecover
Before 11/15/1993Any80 percent
On or after 11/15/1993Working90 percent
On or after 11/15/1993Not working80 percent
Or evacuate the appliance to 4 inches of mercury vacuum.

Example: 4 lb nameplate charge, working compressor, new machine: recover 3.6 lb.

System-dependent recovery equipment (relies on the appliance's own compressor) may not be used on an appliance with a full charge over 15 pounds, unless it is a permanently attached pump-out unit.

Certification: servicing needs Type I (or Universal). Disposing of a small appliance needs no certification, but the recovery rules above still apply.

Who needs which certification to maintain, service, repair or dispose of:

  • Type I: small appliances (servicing)
  • Type II: medium-, high- and very high-pressure appliances, except small appliances and motor vehicle air conditioning
  • Type III: low-pressure appliances
  • Universal: all of the above

Motor vehicle air conditioning for pay is under a separate program (40 CFR part 82, subpart B).

Major repair, oil changes, leak repair

Major repair means removing a compressor, condenser, evaporator or auxiliary heat exchange coil, or uncovering an opening of more than 4 square inches of flow area for more than 15 minutes.

Oil change: evacuate or pressurize the appliance, or the part holding the oil, to no higher than 5 psig before the oil is removed. Or drain the oil into a system receiver that is evacuated or pressurized to no higher than 5 psig.

Leak repair applies to appliances with a full charge of 50 pounds or more of ozone-depleting refrigerant. Trigger leak rate over a 12-month period:

Industrial process refrigeration30 percent
Commercial refrigeration20 percent
Comfort cooling10 percent
All other appliances10 percent

Above the trigger rate, the owner or operator repairs the leaks within 30 days (120 days if an industrial process shutdown is required), or makes a retrofit or retirement plan within 30 days and finishes it within 1 year. Initial and follow-up verification tests follow any repair.

Sources

  • 40 CFR 82.156, Required practices (Cornell LII): evacuation table, small appliance recovery, oil changes, leak exceptions, system-dependent equipment.
  • EPA: Stationary refrigeration service practice requirements: the same evacuation table, small appliance recovery and 5 psig oil rule.
  • 40 CFR 82.152, Definitions (Cornell LII): small appliance, major repair, pressure classes and examples.
  • 40 CFR 82.157, Appropriate disposal and leak repair (Cornell LII) and EPA: Stationary refrigeration leak repair requirements: trigger rates and repair deadlines.
  • 40 CFR 82.161, Technician certification (Cornell LII): which certification covers which appliances. EPA's certification page summarizes Type II without naming medium-pressure appliances; the regulation text includes them.
