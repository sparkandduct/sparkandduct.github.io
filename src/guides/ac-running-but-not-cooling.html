AC running but not cooling: what to check

Work from the easy checks to the ones that need gauges.

Turn power off at the disconnect before opening any panel. Capacitors hold a charge after power is removed, and refrigerant work legally requires EPA Section 608 certification.

1. Thermostat

  • Mode is set to cool and the setpoint is below room temperature.
  • Fan is set to auto. With the fan set to on, the blower runs all the time and the air feels warm between cycles.
  • Batteries are good, if it uses them.

2. Airflow indoors

  • Check the filter first. A clogged filter is the most common cause of poor cooling.
  • Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not covered.
  • Feel the airflow at a register. Weak flow with the blower running points to a blockage or an iced coil.

3. Ice on the indoor coil or the large copper line

Ice means the coil is running below freezing, from low airflow or low refrigerant charge. Switch cooling off, run the fan alone until the ice has fully melted, then fix the airflow problem before testing again. Running an iced system can damage the compressor.

4. Is the outdoor unit running?

  • Nothing running outside: check the breaker and the disconnect, then for 24 V at the contactor coil. No 24 V points back to the thermostat wiring, a tripped condensate float switch or a blown low-voltage fuse on the indoor board.
  • Fan runs but compressor does not: suspect the run capacitor, the contactor or a compressor that has tripped on internal overload from heat.
  • Compressor hums but the fan does not turn: suspect the capacitor or the fan motor.
  • A swollen or leaking capacitor has failed. Confirm with a meter that reads capacitance; a reading more than about 6% below the rating printed on the can is the usual replacement point.

5. Outdoor coil

A condenser coil matted with dirt, grass clippings or cottonwood cannot reject heat. Head pressure climbs and cooling drops. Rinse it from the inside out with a garden hose, with the power off.

6. Both units run and airflow is good

Now it is a refrigerant-side problem and needs gauges and temperature probes:

  • Low charge: low suction pressure, high superheat, and often a frosted coil. Low charge means a leak; find and repair it before adding refrigerant.
  • Restriction: a blocked filter drier or metering device, often with a temperature drop across the restriction.
  • Weak compressor: high suction pressure with low head pressure and low amp draw.

7. It cools, but not enough

On the hottest days a correctly sized system may hold the house only 15 to 20°F below the outdoor temperature. Check for leaking return ducts in a hot attic, which pull in heat, before condemning the equipment.
