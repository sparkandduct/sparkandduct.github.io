Thermostat with no C wire: options and wiring

Check for an unused wire first. If every wire is in use, free one up with the thermostat maker's adapter at the equipment, or pull new cable.

Short answer: the C wire is the other side of the 24 V transformer, from the C terminal on the equipment board to the C terminal on the thermostat. Order of effort: (1) spare wire in the cable, (2) repurpose a wire, (3) maker's power adapter at the furnace or air handler, (4) new cable.

Turn the power off at the equipment before touching any low-voltage wire. A wire touching R and C shorts the transformer and blows the board fuse.

Why a thermostat wants C

A Wi-Fi thermostat has a screen and a radio that run all the time. Without a common wire it has to draw its power through the heating, cooling or fan wires, and some systems cannot give it enough. Google's Nest support page says heat-only, cool-only, zone and heat pump systems need a C wire or the Nest Power Connector, and that low power readings in the thermostat point to a wiring problem. Look at the maker's installation manual for your model; some models run without C on some system types.

Step 1: Look for a spare wire

  1. Pull the old thermostat off the wall and count the conductors in the cable. Thermostat cable usually comes as 18 AWG with two to eight conductors.
  2. Fold any unused wires out. They are often cut short and tucked behind the plate.
  3. At the furnace or air handler, find the same cable at the board. Trace the color, since colors are a convention (see thermostat wire colors).
  4. Land the spare on C at both ends. Label both ends. Check that no conductor is touching another one.

Step 2: Share one wire between two functions

Two makers' manuals show how this is done:

  • Honeywell THP9045 wiring module. It is meant for thermostats that need a 24 V common when there are not enough wires. The thermostat's K terminal runs both the fan and the compressor on one wire; the module receives that signal, splits it, and sends it to the equipment's Y (cooling) and G (fan) terminals. The manual lists the thermostat models it works with, so check that yours is on the list.
  • ecobee Power Extender Kit. For a four-wire setup with no C. The wiring harness mounts within 24 inches of the equipment, a diode goes behind the thermostat on its W/OB and Y terminals, the old W wire is reused as C, and Y and G are combined. It has an ESD warning: touch grounded metal before handling the board.

The exact terminals differ by maker and thermostat, so follow the wiring diagram in the kit's manual for your system type. Do not wire a kit from memory.

What you can lose: ecobee lists accessory control as lost with the kit on its Smart Thermostat Enhanced. With the fan and cooling signals sharing a wire, check the kit's manual for any change to fan-only operation. Run heat, cool and fan from the thermostat before you leave, as the ecobee manual says.

Step 3: Power adapter at the equipment

Some makers sell a small adapter that mounts at the furnace or air handler and supplies power without new wiring to the wall. Google's Nest Power Connector is one: it attaches at the equipment and is sold for Nest thermostats. Compatibility is brand and model specific, so use the maker's compatibility checker.

Step 4: Pull new cable

If the cable is two or three conductors, or the wall allows it, pulling new thermostat cable with enough conductors is the clean fix. Use a cable with at least the conductors you need plus one spare.

After the change

  • Check that the R and C terminals at the thermostat have about 24 V between them (AC volts) and that C is on the equipment board's C terminal, which is the transformer's common.
  • If the board blew a fuse, there was a short between R and C. Find the touching wire before replacing the fuse.
  • If the thermostat still reports low power, look at the transformer. See how to size a control transformer; a transformer loaded near its VA rating may not have room for the thermostat.
  • If the system now does not cool, check the wiring against the AC running but not cooling steps.
