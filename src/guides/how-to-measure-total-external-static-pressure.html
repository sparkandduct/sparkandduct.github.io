How to measure total external static pressure

Read the pressure on the return side after the filter, read the supply side after the coil, add the two without signs, and compare the total with the maximum on the unit's rating plate.

Short answer: total external static pressure (TESP) = |return reading| + |supply reading|, in inches of water column (in. w.c.). Example: return −0.30 and supply +0.45 gives 0.75 in. w.c. If the rating plate says 0.50 maximum, the ducts, filter or coil are restricting the blower.

What you need

  • A digital manometer or a magnetic-gauge type that reads in inches of water column, with two hose ports.
  • Static pressure probes, or a drill and a plug for each test hole. Fieldpiece advises drilling outside the manufactured box so you do not puncture the coil, blower or heat exchanger.
  • The rating plate or installation manual, for the maximum external static pressure and the blower speed it applies to.

Step 1: Set the system up

  1. Put a clean filter in. Goodman's manual takes readings with clean filters, and a dirty or missing filter changes the answer.
  2. If an evaporator coil or electronic air cleaner is part of the system, leave it in place. Goodman's manual says the readings must include those components.
  3. Open all the registers and dampers you normally run with.
  4. Set the blower to the speed you are testing. Fieldpiece suggests the highest airflow speed and about three minutes to settle before you read.
  5. Zero the manometer in free air before the probes go in. Fieldpiece says to re-zero before each use because field manometers drift.

Step 2: Take the two readings

  1. Return. Drill a test hole in the return duct or the unit's return side, after the filter and before the blower. Fieldpiece puts the return reading "typically after the filter". Goodman's manual says "at the inlet of the furnace".
  2. Supply. Drill in the supply plenum, after the coil and clear of the coil's frame. On an upflow furnace that is above the coil; on a downflow, below it.
  3. Face the probe opening so it is perpendicular to the airflow, as Fieldpiece says, or use a probe with an arrow that points into the airflow. Hold it still. Do not poke it into a duct fitting or elbow.
  4. Read both. Return is negative and supply is positive. Many digital manometers read both ports against the room at once and show the sum.
  5. Plug the holes with plugs or foil tape when you finish.

Step 3: Add and compare

Drop the signs and add. Goodman's manual shows the same total as a difference of the two signed numbers: −0.1 return and +0.3 supply is 0.4 in. w.c.

The plate value varies with the equipment. Fieldpiece says a system runs most efficiently at or below 0.5 in. w.c., but also that the best value depends on the manufacturer. Use your unit's plate. Goodman's manual says that if TESP exceeds the plate maximum, check for closed dampers or registers and ductwork that is undersized, oversized or poorly laid out.

Reading the result

What you seeWhat it points to
Return reading much more negative than the supply is positiveReturn side: dirty or too-small filter, undersized return duct or grille, closed return damper. (General practice.)
Supply reading much higher than the returnSupply side: closed registers or dampers, undersized or kinked duct, a blocked coil. (General practice.)
TESP low and the system is not moving airLeaks, separated duct, a missing filter or low blower speed, per Fieldpiece. Check the blower speed tap too.

Pressure and airflow are linked. A blower delivers less air as static rises, and the manufacturer's blower table shows how many CFM at each static for each speed. Once you have TESP and the blower speed, the table gives the airflow, which you can check against the furnace with the temperature rise method and the airflow calculator. High static plus a high temperature rise confirms the air is being choked. Check the filter first: it is the cheapest fix. Then see the AC running but not cooling guide if a frozen coil is part of the problem.
