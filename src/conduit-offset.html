How it is worked out

distance between bends = offset depth × multiplier

shrink = offset depth × shrink per inch

AngleMultiplierShrink per inch of depth
10°6.01/16 in.
22.5°2.63/16 in.
30°2.01/4 in.
45°1.43/8 in.
60°1.21/2 in.

These are the rounded figures the trade uses. The multiplier is the cosecant of the bend angle, 1 ÷ sin(angle), and the shrink per inch is tan(angle ÷ 2). The line under the result shows the unrounded trigonometry so you can see how far the rounding moves the answer; at 10° the usual 6.0 multiplier is about 4% over the exact 5.76.

Shrink is how much the offset shortens the reach of the conduit. If the obstruction is 40 in. from the end and you bend a 6 in. offset at 30°, the shrink is 6 × 1/4 = 1.5 in., so the first mark goes at 41.5 in. The second mark is 6 × 2.0 = 12 in. back toward the end you measured from, at 29.5 in. Leave the distance box empty if you only need the spacing.

Fractions are rounded to the nearest 1/16 in. Both bends must be made in the same plane or the offset comes out twisted (a dog leg). Shallower angles pull wire more easily and take more room; 30° is the common middle ground because the multiplier is a round 2.

Check the marks on your own bender before cutting to length: makers differ on which mark lines up for an offset, and large conduit bent on a mechanical or hydraulic bender follows that bender's own chart. The total of all bends between pull points is limited by the electrical code, and an offset counts as two bends.
