Worked example (filled in)

A three-phase pump motor read at the terminals. The numbers are made up to show the arithmetic; the voltage readings are the example used in the DOE tip sheet listed under Sources.

Voltage unbalance

L1-L2, L2-L3, L1-L3462 V, 463 V, 455 V
V1. Average (462 + 463 + 455) ÷ 3460 V
V2. Largest difference from V1 (460 − 455)5 V
V3. Unbalance % (5 ÷ 460 × 100)1.1 %

DOE, citing NEMA MG 1, recommends that unbalance at the motor terminals not exceed 1 percent, and says more than 1 percent requires derating the motor per the NEMA derating figure. This example is just over, so you would look for the cause (see the checklist).

Polarization index

Terminal T1 to frame, test voltage held for 10 minutes1 min: 800 MΩ   10 min: 1,600 MΩ
Polarization index (1,600 ÷ 800)2.0

What counts as an acceptable index or reading depends on the winding insulation class, voltage rating and edition of IEEE 43, so this sheet does not print limits. Judge your readings against the motor maker's data and your own earlier readings of the same motor.

Check before you hand it in

  • □ Power is locked out and tested dead before any resistance or megohm reading.
  • □ Drive, starter and power factor capacitors are disconnected before the megohmmeter is connected, and the winding is discharged after the test.
  • □ The test voltage is not above what the motor maker allows.
  • □ All three voltages are read at the motor terminals, not at the disconnect.
  • □ Both megohm readings of a polarization index were taken at the same winding temperature.
  • □ If voltage unbalance is over 1 percent, check single-phase loads sharing the supply, the transformer bank, and any power factor correction.

Sources

  • US Department of Energy, Motor Systems Tip Sheet #7: Eliminate Voltage Unbalance: the NEMA definition of percent voltage unbalance (100 times the largest deviation from the average, divided by the average), the 462, 463 and 455 V example, and the 1 percent recommendation.
  • Industrial Monitor Direct: NEMA voltage unbalance and motor derating: the same method and the 1 percent figure (its printed formula has an error; the worked example matches DOE).
  • The Pump & Motor Works: Electric motor insulation resistance testing per IEEE 43: polarization index as the 10-minute resistance divided by the 1-minute resistance.
  • InsulationTesting.com: Polarization index: the same definition, with the readings taken at one test voltage and one temperature.
