How it is worked out

Friction rate is the pressure you can spend per 100 feet of duct. It comes from the static pressure left over after the equipment and components take their share, spread over the longest run:

friction rate = available static pressure × 100 ÷ total effective length

Total effective length is the straight length plus the equivalent length of every fitting on the longest supply or return run. Fittings are often most of it. Available static pressure is the blower's rated external static pressure minus the pressure drop of the filter, coil, grilles and any other components.

Round duct friction for galvanized steel at standard air follows:

friction rate = 0.109136 × CFM1.9 ÷ diameter5.02

Solved for the diameter: diameter = (0.109136 × CFM1.9 ÷ friction rate)1 ÷ 5.02 inches. Air speed is CFM ÷ duct area in square feet.

Worked example: a blower rated for 0.50 in. w.c. leaves 0.31 in. w.c. after the filter, coil and registers, and the longest run has a total effective length of 424 ft. Friction rate = 0.31 × 100 ÷ 424 = 0.073 in. w.c. per 100 ft. For an 800 CFM trunk, 8001.9 = 328,000 (rounded), 0.109136 × 328,000 = 35,800, and 35,800 ÷ 0.073 = 490,000. The 5.02nd root of 490,000 is 13.6 in. The next size up, 14 in., gives 0.063 in. w.c. per 100 ft at about 748 ft/min. A 13 in. duct would run at 0.092 and about 868 ft/min, which is over the 0.073 you have to spend. The 0.31 in. w.c. and 424 ft come from a worked example in an Energy Vanguard article and the other numbers are calculated here.

Using it in the field

  • There is no one friction rate for every house. The right one comes from the blower's static pressure, the components and the length of the longest run. Do the available static pressure step each time.
  • Size every branch from the same friction rate, with its own CFM. The longest run sets the rate; shorter runs then have pressure to spare, which a balancing damper uses up.
  • This is round, smooth galvanized duct. Flex duct, duct board and rectangular duct have different friction, and rectangular duct needs an equivalent round diameter. Check the maker's chart for flex.
  • The calculator does not include fittings. Add their equivalent lengths to the total effective length before you work out the friction rate.
  • Check the air speed against the limits in Manual D or your local code. This tool does not set one, because the limit depends on the duct type, the room and the noise you can accept.
  • Measure the finished system. The total external static pressure guide shows how, and the temperature rise guide shows what a restricted duct does to heat output.
  • Airflow rules of thumb and the CFM from a temperature difference are on the airflow calculator.
