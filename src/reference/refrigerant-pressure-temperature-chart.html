Bubble is the pressure at which liquid starts to boil at that temperature. Dew is where the last of the vapor is there. R-32 is a single fluid, so it has one column. R-410A is a blend that glides by only a fraction of a degree, so its two columns differ by about 1 psi and either one works in the field.

Which column to use

  • Superheat: subtract the dew temperature at the suction pressure from the suction line temperature.
  • Subcooling: subtract the liquid line temperature from the bubble temperature at the liquid line pressure.

R-454B glides about 2°F to 3°F across its range in the table. Use the wrong column and superheat or subcooling is off by that much, enough to move a TXV subcooling target of 10°F to 12°F. The chart is by temperature; to go the other way, find your gauge pressure down the column and read across to the temperature.

Worked examples

R-454B superheat. Suction gauge reads 120 psig and the suction line is 55°F. At 120 psig the dew temperature is about 45.7°F (the chart shows 118 psig dew at 45°F and 130 psig at 50°F). Superheat = 55 − 45.7 = 9.3°F. Using the bubble temperature of 43.1°F would read 11.9°F, which is wrong.

R-454B subcooling. Liquid line gauge reads 330 psig and the liquid line is 95°F. At 330 psig the bubble temperature is about 106.0°F. Subcooling = 106.0 − 95 = 11.0°F. The dew temperature (108.6°F) would give 13.6°F.

For R-410A and R-22 the superheat and subcooling calculator does this lookup for you. It does not include R-32 or R-454B, so use this chart for those and do the subtraction by hand.

Reading the chart in the field

  • At the same temperature, R-454B runs about 5% below R-410A in gauge pressure, and R-32 runs about 2% above it. A gauge set that reads R-410A pressures on an R-454B unit will point to the wrong charge.
  • Gauges read pressure above the local air pressure. These values are for sea level; at altitude the same refrigerant temperature shows a slightly different gauge reading.
  • Charge targets come from the unit’s data plate and manual, not this chart. The chart only converts a pressure to a temperature.

Where the numbers come from

The values were computed with CoolProp reference equations of state (R-32 pure fluid; R-410A as 50/50 R-32 and R-125 by mass; R-454B as 68.9% R-32 and 31.1% R-1234yf). They were checked against the manufacturer chart below, which lists the same fluids from 38°F below zero to 150°F: R-410A and R-32 agree within 0.1 psi at every point compared. R-454B agrees within about 0.5 psi up to 120°F and within about 1.5 psi at 150°F. Treat the last digit as approximate and confirm against the chart from the maker of your refrigerant for any reading near a limit.
