Electrical box fill calculator

Adds up the cubic inches the NEC 314.16 method requires and compares them with the volume stamped in the box. Conductors in AWG 14 to 6.

Count conductors that enter the box from outside. Pigtails and jumpers that start and end inside the box are not counted.

How it is worked out

Each item gets an allowance in cubic inches based on the conductor size in Table 314.16(B). Add them up and the sum must not exceed the box volume.

ConductorAllowance
14 AWG2.00 in³
12 AWG2.25 in³
10 AWG2.50 in³
8 AWG3.00 in³
6 AWG5.00 in³
  • Every conductor that enters the box counts once.
  • All internal cable clamps together count once, at the allowance of the largest conductor in the box. Clamps outside the box add nothing.
  • Each support fitting counts once at the largest conductor allowance.
  • Each device yoke counts twice at the largest conductor connected to it. This calculator uses the largest conductor in the box, which is the cautious choice.
  • All equipment grounding conductors together count once, at the largest one. Under the 2023 edition, check the text for an extra quarter allowance for each ground beyond four; the calculator adds it when you pick 2023.

Worked example. Two 14 AWG cables (two black, two white, two bare) feed a switch in an 18 in³ single-gang box with internal clamps. The two bare grounds count as one.

  • 4 conductors × 2.00 = 8.00
  • 1 ground × 2.00 = 2.00
  • Clamps: 1 × 2.00 = 2.00
  • 1 yoke × 2 × 2.00 = 4.00
  • Total 16.00 in³ against 18.0 in³ marked, so it fits with 2.00 in³ to spare.

Swap those four conductors for 12 AWG and the same box needs 4 × 2.25 + 2.25 + 2.25 + 4.50 = 18.00 in³, exactly full.

Use the volume marked on the box; the code also lists standard volumes for unmarked metal boxes. This calculator leaves out luminaire fixture wires, conductors smaller than 14 AWG and anything with its own rule, such as the 300.14 free-length rule for conductors that pass through. For those, read 314.16 in your adopted code book. To size the feed to the box, see voltage drop and conduit fill.
