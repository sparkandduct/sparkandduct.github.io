How to measure furnace temperature rise

One of the quickest checks of whether a furnace is moving enough air.

The airflow calculator turns heat output and temperature rise into CFM.

What it tells you

Temperature rise is the difference between the air entering the furnace and the air leaving it. Every furnace nameplate gives an acceptable range, for example 35 to 65 °F. A rise above the range means too little airflow, which overheats the heat exchanger and trips the limit switch. A rise below the range means too much airflow or too little heat, which can cause condensation in a furnace that is not built for it.

How to measure it

  1. Set the thermostat to call for heat and let the furnace run for at least ten minutes so temperatures settle.
  2. Measure return air temperature in the return duct close to the furnace.
  3. Measure supply air temperature in the supply plenum, far enough downstream that the probe cannot "see" the heat exchanger. Radiant heat from the exchanger gives a false high reading.
  4. Subtract return from supply.

Take both readings with the same thermometer where you can, and make sure the filter is clean and the registers are open before you start.

Turning rise into airflow

CFM = output BTU/h ÷ (1.08 × rise)

Use output, which is the input rating multiplied by the efficiency.

Worked example

An 80,000 BTU/h input furnace at 95 % efficiency has an output of 76,000 BTU/h. Measured rise is 55 °F.

  • CFM = 76,000 ÷ (1.08 × 55) = 76,000 ÷ 59.4 = about 1,280 CFM.

For electric heat, convert kilowatts to BTU/h by multiplying by 3,412 first.

If the rise is too high

  • Dirty filter or a filter that is too restrictive for the system.
  • Blower speed set too low for heating.
  • Dirty blower wheel or a dirty evaporator coil above the furnace.
  • Closed or blocked registers, or ductwork that is too small.
  • Furnace over-fired: check gas pressure and orifice size.

If the rise is too low

  • Blower speed set too high.
  • Furnace under-fired: low gas pressure or wrong orifices.
  • On a two-stage furnace, the reading was taken on low fire and compared with the high-fire range.

The 1.08 factor is for standard air at sea level and falls with altitude. Gas pressure and combustion work should be done by a qualified technician.
