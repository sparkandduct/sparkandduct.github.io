How to size a fire alarm battery

The calculation every fire alarm submittal needs, worked through by hand.

Want the answer without the arithmetic? Use the fire alarm battery calculator.

What the battery has to do

When primary power fails, the batteries must carry the system in standby for a set time and then still have enough left to sound the alarm. NFPA 72 sets the common case at 24 hours of standby followed by 5 minutes of alarm. Voice evacuation systems need 15 minutes of alarm.

Step 1: List every device and its current

For each item the panel powers, find two numbers on the data sheet: standby current and alarm current, both in milliamps. Include the panel itself, annunciators, modules, detectors and notification appliances. Multiply each by its quantity.

Step 2: Total the standby and alarm loads

Add up the standby column and the alarm column separately, then convert to amps by dividing by 1,000.

Step 3: Convert to amp-hours

Ah = standby amps × standby hours + alarm amps × alarm hours

Five minutes is 5 ÷ 60 = 0.083 hours.

Step 4: Add the margin

Multiply by 1.2. NFPA 72 requires the calculated capacity to include a 20% safety margin, which covers battery aging.

Step 5: Pick the next size up

Choose the next standard battery at or above the result, then confirm that the panel's charger is listed to charge a battery that large and that it fits in the cabinet.

Worked example

DeviceQtyStandby mAAlarm mA
Control panel1120250
Smoke detector200.3 each = 62 each = 40
Horn strobe10095 each = 950
Total126 mA1,240 mA
  • Standby: 0.126 A × 24 h = 3.02 Ah
  • Alarm: 1.24 A × 0.083 h = 0.10 Ah
  • Total: 3.13 Ah
  • With margin: 3.13 × 1.2 = 3.75 Ah

The next standard size is 4.5 Ah, although most installers would fit the common 7 Ah pair. The currents here are examples; use the data sheet values for the equipment you are installing.

Common mistakes

  • Using the strobe's nominal current. Strobe current depends on the candela setting. Use the figure for the setting you will install.
  • Leaving out auxiliary loads. Door holders, relays and remote annunciators fed from the panel all count.
  • Doubling the amp-hours for two batteries. Two 12 V batteries in series make 24 V at the amp-hour rating of one.
  • Ignoring the local requirement. Some jurisdictions and some system types need more standby time. Ask before you submit.
