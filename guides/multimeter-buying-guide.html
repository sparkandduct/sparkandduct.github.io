What to look for in a multimeter

Buy on safety rating first, then on the measurements your trade needs.

Safety comes first

  • Category rating. Meters are rated by where on an electrical system they are safe to use. CAT II is for plug-in loads, CAT III is for panels, feeders and fixed equipment, and CAT IV is for the service entrance and outdoors. For work in panels and on equipment, look for CAT III 600 V at a minimum.
  • Independent testing. A rating printed on the case means little without a mark from a testing lab such as UL, ETL or CSA.
  • Fused current inputs. The amp jacks should be protected by high-rupture fuses, not small glass ones.
  • Leads. Leads carry their own category rating. Replace cracked or loose ones; they are the cheapest part of the kit and the part in your hands.

Features worth paying for

  • True RMS. Gives correct AC readings on the distorted waveforms produced by variable speed drives, electronic ballasts and inverter equipment. An averaging meter can read well off on those.
  • Low impedance mode (LoZ). Loads the circuit slightly so that "ghost" voltages induced on unconnected wires collapse to zero. Saves a lot of confusion on long runs and in shared conduits.
  • A loud, fast continuity beeper. Sounds trivial until you are ringing out forty conductors.
  • Backlight and a holster or magnet. You will use these every day.

By trade

HVAC

  • Microamps DC for flame sensor current, which is typically only a few microamps.
  • Capacitance for run and start capacitors.
  • Temperature with a thermocouple input.
  • A clamp for motor and compressor current. Many HVAC technicians carry a clamp meter with these functions built in as their main meter.

Fire alarm and low voltage

  • Good DC voltage resolution for 12 and 24 V circuits and for voltage drop checks at the end of a run.
  • Resistance up to tens of megohms for end-of-line resistors and for chasing ground faults.
  • Milliamps DC for device current and 4 to 20 mA loops.

Electrical

  • CAT III 1000 V or CAT IV 600 V if you work in commercial or industrial gear.
  • A clamp meter alongside the multimeter for load checks.
  • A separate non-contact tester as a first check only. Always confirm with the meter on a known live source before and after testing a circuit as dead.

What not to pay for

Bluetooth logging, graphing screens and very high accuracy figures matter in a lab and rarely in the field. A mid-range true RMS meter with the right category rating from an established brand will outlast several cheaper ones.
