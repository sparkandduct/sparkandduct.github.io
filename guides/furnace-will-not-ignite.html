Furnace will not ignite: troubleshooting by stage

Watch one start-up from the thermostat call. Where the sequence stops tells you which part to test.

Short answer: if the burners light and go out after a few seconds, clean the flame sensor and check its microamp reading. If nothing lights, find the last thing that happened (inducer, igniter glow, gas valve click) and test the next step. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility.

The start-up sequence and typical timings

Most modern hot-surface-ignition furnaces run the same order. The times below are from two Bryant installation manuals (a 912S and a 345MAV); your furnace may differ, so the manual and the wiring diagram inside the door win.

  1. The thermostat calls for heat. The control checks that the pressure switches are open.
  2. The inducer starts. When it is up to speed the pressure switch closes.
  3. Prepurge: 15 seconds.
  4. Igniter warm-up: 17 seconds.
  5. The gas valve opens. About 5 seconds later the control looks for flame for 2 seconds.
  6. Flame proven: the igniter shuts off and the blower starts after a delay (25 seconds in one manual, 66 seconds in the other).

If flame is not proven, the control closes the gas valve and tries again, up to three more times in those manuals, then locks out. Lockout clears after three hours or when power to the furnace is interrupted for a moment. Other makers use other retry counts and reset times.

Flame sensor: clean it, then measure it

The sensor rod sits in the flame and passes a tiny DC current through it to ground. The control reads that current as proof of flame. A film on the rod cuts the current, so the burners light and drop out after a couple of seconds. Cleaning usually fixes it: pull the rod, rub it lightly with a fine abrasive pad, wipe it, and refit it so it sits in the flame as before.

To measure, put a DC microamp meter in series with the sensor wire (take the wire off the rod and put the meter between the wire and the rod), then run a heating cycle. Two Goodman service manuals give these ranges for their integrated ignition controls:

  • GMES8 manual: 3 to 10 µA is normal. 1 to 3 µA is low and flashes an amber light. Below 1 µA the furnace shuts down.
  • GMSS9 manual: 1 to 4 µA.

The two manuals disagree, so there is no single number. Use the one in your furnace's manual. A low reading with a clean rod points to a loose or corroded connection, the wrong sensor-to-burner gap or a poor ground. The GMSS9 manual also warns that contaminated combustion air can leave a nearly invisible coating on the rod.

Reset and retries

Interrupting power for a moment clears most lockouts so you can watch another start. Do not keep resetting a furnace that fails the same way. A furnace that keeps locking out on a safety switch is telling you something is wrong; find it before another reset.
