Heat pump blowing cold air in heating mode: what to check

First rule out defrost and the thermostat. Then check airflow and the outdoor unit. Reversing valve and charge come last.

Short answer: cool air for a few minutes with steam or a stopped outdoor fan is a defrost cycle and is normal. Air that stays cool after the thermostat is confirmed on Heat, with the filter clean and the outdoor coil clear, points to the auxiliary heat, the reversing valve or the refrigerant charge, and needs gauges.

Is it defrost?

In cold, damp weather the outdoor coil frosts over. The unit melts the frost by running in cooling mode for a short time, so the indoor coil cools and the air from the registers feels cool unless backup heat comes on.

Two manufacturer manuals describe the same defrost sequence with different numbers:

  • The reversing valve switches, the outdoor fan stops, and the electric heat comes on.
  • Bryant (R-22 service manual): defrost only runs when outdoor air is below 50°F, starts when the coil thermistor is at or below 30°F and the timed interval has accumulated, and ends when the coil reaches 80°F or after 10 minutes.
  • Bard (W24HY-A): the timer runs when the coil is at 30°F or below, with a choice of 30, 60 or 90 minutes (60 from the factory). Defrost ends at about 57°F coil temperature, or after 8 minutes if it runs long.

Lennox gives 5 to 15 minutes as a usual defrost length. If the cool air lasts longer than that, or the outdoor coil is still iced over after a cycle, defrost is not working. Look at the defrost control, the coil sensor and the reversing valve.

Auxiliary heat during defrost is how a house avoids cold air from the registers. If the strip heat is off, tripped or failed, defrost feels like cold air.

Check order when it is not defrost

CheckWhat you are looking for
ThermostatMode on Heat, not Cool. Fan on Auto, since a fan set to On pushes air through when the unit is off. Setpoint above room temperature. Lennox suggests raising it at least 5° to test.
Filter and registersDirty filter or closed registers. Lennox suggests changing filters every 1 to 3 months.
Outdoor unitLeaves, snow or ice on the coil, and no clearance. Lennox recommends at least two feet clear on all sides.
Is the compressor running?Outdoor fan turning and compressor humming, or off? A dead outdoor unit that leaves only strip heat is a different fault from a running unit with no heat.
Auxiliary heatDoes the strip heat come on with the thermostat set well above room temperature? Breaker, sequencers, limit.
Reversing valveUnit runs in heating mode but acts like cooling: indoor coil cold, outdoor coil warm. Check for 24 V at the valve coil (O or B terminal, depending on the brand). A valve with power that does not shift is stuck or leaking internally.
ChargeLow refrigerant means weak heat and frosting. Check with gauges and the superheat and subcooling targets; do not add refrigerant without finding the leak.

Use the superheat and subcooling calculator once gauges are on. Refrigerant work needs EPA Section 608 certification.

Cool air that is normal

A heat pump moves heat instead of burning fuel, so its supply air is cooler than a furnace's. Air near body temperature can feel cold on the hand. Lennox notes that efficiency drops as outdoor temperatures fall. Measure the supply and return temperature rather than going by feel.
