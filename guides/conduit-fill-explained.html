Conduit fill explained

How many wires fit, and why the limit changes with the count.

The conduit fill calculator does this for EMT with THHN conductors and cables.

The rule

The NEC limits how much of a conduit's cross-section the conductors may occupy, so wire can be pulled without damage and heat can escape. The limits are in Chapter 9, Table 1:

ConductorsMaximum fill
153 %
231 %
3 or more40 %

Two conductors get the lowest limit because two round wires side by side tend to jam across the conduit's diameter during a pull.

Working it out by hand

  1. Find the conduit's total internal area in Chapter 9, Table 4, for the conduit type and trade size.
  2. Find each conductor's area in Table 5, for its insulation type and size.
  3. Add the conductor areas together.
  4. Divide by the conduit area and compare with the limit for that number of conductors.

Worked example: THHN

Three 12 AWG THHN conductors in 1/2" EMT:

  • Each conductor: 0.0133 in², so three are 0.0399 in².
  • 1/2" EMT: 0.304 in².
  • Fill: 0.0399 ÷ 0.304 = 13.1 %, well under 40 %.

Worked example: low voltage cables

Cables are not in Table 5, so work from the outside diameter on the cable's data sheet: area = π × diameter² ÷ 4.

Four cables of 0.25" outside diameter in 3/4" EMT:

  • Each cable: 3.1416 × 0.25² ÷ 4 = 0.0491 in², so four are 0.196 in².
  • 3/4" EMT: 0.533 in².
  • Fill: 0.196 ÷ 0.533 = 36.8 %, which passes at 40 %.

A multiconductor cable counts as one conductor for the percentage. For a flat or oval cable, use the larger dimension as the diameter.

Things fill does not cover

  • Ampacity adjustment. More than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway means derating their ampacity. That is a separate rule and often the tighter one.
  • Pulling difficulty. A legal fill can still be a miserable pull on a long run with several bends. Going up a size is cheap.
  • Short nipples. Nipples 24 inches or shorter are allowed 60 % fill.

For estimating and study only. Check everything against the current code, the manufacturer's data and the authority having jurisdiction before you build or repair.
