How it is worked out

PoE runs DC through two pairs (one pair out, one pair back) or through four. The two wires of a pair carry the same current side by side, so each pair is two conductors in parallel.

conductor resistance = 1.724 × 10-8 Ω·m × length ÷ wire area, corrected by 0.393% per °C away from 20°C.

Wire area comes from the AWG diameter, 0.127 mm × 92(36 − AWG) ÷ 39. A pairset is two pairs in series, each pair two conductors in parallel, so its loop resistance equals the resistance of one conductor over the full length. With four pairs, two pairsets are in parallel and the loop resistance is halved.

The device takes a set power, so the current is found from P = V×I − I²×R, which gives I = (V − √(V² − 4×R×P)) ÷ (2×R). Voltage drop is I × R, voltage at the device is V minus the drop, and cable loss is I² × R.

Worked example: a Class 4 device (25.5 W) on a 50 V switch, 100 m of solid 24 AWG, two pairs. Conductor resistance is 8.42 Ω. I = (50 − √(2500 − 4 × 8.42 × 25.5)) ÷ (2 × 8.42) = (50 − 40.53) ÷ 16.84 = 0.563 A. Drop is 0.563 × 8.42 = 4.74 V, so the device sees 45.26 V. Cable loss is 2.67 W and the switch supplies 28.17 W. The same run on four pairs for a 51 W Class 6 device is 4.21 Ω, 1.127 A, the same 4.74 V drop, 5.35 W of loss and 56.3 W from the switch.

Because the device holds its power constant, a long run draws more current at the lower voltage, so loss grows faster than length.

Classes 1 to 4 can use two pairs. Class 5 and above need four. The standard guarantees operation up to 12.5 Ω of DC loop resistance per pairset (6.25 Ω with four pairs in parallel), which is the worst case for a 100 m channel.

Using it in the field

  • The standard limit is on the whole channel, so add the patch cords at both ends to the run length. The result is for the cable you enter, not for connectors or bad terminations.
  • Solid 24 AWG reaches 12.5 Ω of one conductor at about 148 m at 20°C, so for good copper the 100 m Ethernet channel length is the tighter limit.
  • Heat adds resistance. A bundle of cables all carrying PoE runs warm; enter the temperature and see the change (a cable at 60°C is about 16% higher in resistance than at 20°C).
  • Copper-clad aluminum cable is not covered here. Its resistance is higher, and this tool assumes solid copper.
  • This is the drop in the cable. The device's own minimum input voltage still applies; the 802.3af range at the device is 37 to 57 V and the 802.3at range is 42.5 to 57 V.
  • For non-PoE low voltage runs such as 24 V circuits, use the voltage drop calculator. For fire alarm notification circuits see NAC voltage drop.
