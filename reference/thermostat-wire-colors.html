Thermostat wire colors

The usual color for each terminal, and what the terminal does.

Color is a convention, not a rule. Installers run out of colors and substitute. Always confirm by looking at which terminal each wire lands on at the furnace or air handler board.

Conventional systems

TerminalUsual colorWhat it does
RRed24 V power from the transformer.
Rc / RhRedSeparate 24 V feeds for cooling and heating when there are two transformers. With one transformer they are jumpered together.
CBlue or blackCommon: the other side of the transformer. Needed by most smart thermostats.
GGreenIndoor blower fan.
YYellowCooling: energizes the outdoor unit's contactor.
Y2Light blue or pinkSecond stage cooling.
WWhiteHeat.
W2BrownSecond stage heat.

Heat pumps

TerminalUsual colorWhat it does
YYellowCompressor, in both heating and cooling.
O/BOrange (O) or dark blue (B)Reversing valve. O energizes it in cooling, which most brands use. B energizes it in heating.
W2 / AuxWhiteAuxiliary heat: the electric strips or furnace that help the heat pump.
EVariesEmergency heat: runs the backup heat alone, with the compressor locked out.

On a heat pump, blue is often used for common as well, so a system with a B terminal needs extra care. Check the board, not the color.

Other terminals you may see

  • S1 / S2: outdoor or remote temperature sensor.
  • L: fault indicator from a heat pump.
  • DH or DEHUM: dehumidification signal to a variable speed blower.
  • 1, 2, A, B, D: communicating systems that send data instead of switching 24 V. These do not follow the chart above and cannot be mixed with a standard thermostat.

Before you disconnect anything

  • Turn the power off at the furnace or air handler. Shorting R to C blows the low-voltage fuse on the board.
  • Photograph the old thermostat's wiring with the terminal letters visible.
  • Thick wires on wire nuts mean a line-voltage thermostat (120 or 240 V) for electric baseboard heat. None of this page applies to those.

For estimating and study only. Check everything against the current code, the manufacturer's data and the authority having jurisdiction before you build or repair.
