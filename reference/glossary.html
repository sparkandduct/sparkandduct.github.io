Glossary of trade terms

Short, plain definitions.

Fire alarm and low voltage

AHJ
Authority having jurisdiction. The person or office with the power to approve an installation, usually the fire marshal or building inspector.
Candela
The unit of light intensity used to rate strobes. A higher candela setting covers a larger room and draws more current.
End-of-line resistor (EOL)
A resistor at the last device on a circuit. The panel watches for its value to confirm the wiring is intact.
FACP
Fire alarm control panel.
Ground fault
An unintended connection between a circuit conductor and earth ground. See how to find one.
IDC
Initiating device circuit. A conventional zone circuit carrying detectors and pull stations.
NAC
Notification appliance circuit. The circuit that powers horns, strobes and speakers.
Plenum rated
Cable listed for use in air-handling spaces, such as above a ceiling used as a return. Marked with a P at the end of the listing, as in FPLP or CMP.
SLC
Signaling line circuit. The data loop that addressable devices communicate on.
Supervisory signal
A signal that something the fire protection depends on is off normal, such as a closed sprinkler valve. Different from an alarm and from a trouble.
Trouble signal
A signal that the fire alarm system itself has a fault, such as an open circuit, ground fault or low battery.

Electrical

Ampacity
The current a conductor can carry continuously without exceeding its temperature rating.
AWG
American Wire Gauge. Smaller numbers are larger wires. Sizes larger than 4/0 are given in kcmil.
Circular mil
A unit of conductor cross-section: the area of a circle one thousandth of an inch across.
EMT
Electrical metallic tubing. Thin-wall steel conduit joined with fittings instead of threads.
High leg
The phase on a four-wire delta system that measures about 208 V to neutral. Marked orange.
Line and load
Line is the supply side of a device; load is the side feeding the equipment.
VFD
Variable frequency drive. Controls an AC motor's speed by changing the frequency of its supply.
Voltage drop
The voltage lost in a conductor's resistance between the source and the load. See the calculator.

HVAC

BTU
British thermal unit. The heat needed to raise one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit. Equipment is rated in BTU per hour.
CFM
Cubic feet per minute. The volume of air a system moves.
Contactor
A relay that switches line voltage to the compressor and outdoor fan when its 24 V coil is energized.
Flame sensor
A rod in the burner flame that passes a few microamps to prove the burners are lit.
Inducer
The small blower that draws combustion gases through a furnace's heat exchanger and into the vent.
Static pressure
The resistance to airflow in a duct system, measured in inches of water column.
Subcooling
How far the liquid refrigerant leaving the condenser is below its saturation temperature. Used to check charge on systems with an expansion valve.
Superheat
How far the refrigerant vapor leaving the evaporator is above its saturation temperature. Used to check charge on fixed-orifice systems and to protect the compressor from liquid.
Temperature rise
Supply air temperature minus return air temperature across a furnace. See how to measure it.
Ton
A cooling capacity of 12,000 BTU per hour.
TXV
Thermostatic expansion valve. A metering device that adjusts refrigerant flow to hold a steady superheat.
